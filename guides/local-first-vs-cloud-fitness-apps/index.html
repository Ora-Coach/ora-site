Privacy

Local-first vs cloud fitness apps: what privacy actually means.

“We take your privacy seriously” appears in every fitness app’s policy, including the ones that sell your data. The difference that matters isn’t the slogan — it’s the architecture: where your data lives, what leaves your device, and who decides.

The short answer

Cloud-first apps store your training data primarily on their servers; your phone is a window onto their database, and the company controls (and can change) what happens to the data. Local-first apps treat your device as the primary home: logging and history work without a permanent network dependency, and anything that leaves — sync, sharing, backups — is explicit and scoped. Local-first is a spectrum, not a badge; the questions below matter more than the label.

Why training data is more sensitive than it looks

  • Routine and location. When and where you train, every week, for years. The 2018 Strava heatmap incident showed aggregate workout data exposing military base locations — individual patterns are no less revealing.
  • Health information. Body weight over time, injuries, pain notes, nutrition — data most people would treat as medical if a doctor held it.
  • A behavioral record. Years of logs describe your discipline, your setbacks, your body. That’s valuable to advertisers and insurers precisely because it’s honest.

None of this means cloud apps are malicious — many are well-run. It means the default architecture decides what’s possible later: data that never left your device can’t be breached from a server, repurposed by an acquirer, or quietly folded into a new monetization plan.

What local-first is — and isn’t

Local-first is not the same as an offline mode. Offline mode is a cache: the cloud database is still the source of truth, and the app is limited until it reconnects. Local-first inverts that — the device is the source of truth, the network is the optional layer. It also isn’t all-or-nothing: a coach reviewing your week, a report shared with a partner, or a backup all involve data leaving the device. The honest version of local-first is that those flows exist, are opt-in, scoped, and revocable, and are labeled so you know which surface you’re on.

Questions to ask any fitness app (including us)

  1. Does logging and history work with the network off — indefinitely, not just as a cache?
  2. Can I export everything, in a format another tool can read, without emailing support?
  3. Can I delete my data and is deletion real, not a soft flag?
  4. When data does leave the device (sync, coach sharing, reports), is it explicit, scoped to what’s needed, and revocable?
  5. Is there third-party ad or analytics tracking inside the app, and is the answer written down where I can check it?
  6. What’s the business model? If it isn’t subscriptions or purchases, the data usually is the model.

FAQ

What does local-first mean in a fitness app?
Your device is the primary home of your data: logging works without a permanent network dependency, and anything that leaves — sync, sharing, backups — is explicit and under your control. It’s not the same as an offline cache in front of a cloud database.

Is training data really sensitive?
More than it looks: routines and locations, body weight and injuries, years of behavioral history. Aggregate workout data has already caused real incidents — the 2018 Strava heatmap exposed military base locations.
