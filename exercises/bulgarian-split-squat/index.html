Bulgarian Split Squat

The Bulgarian split squat is a single-leg squat with the back foot up on a bench. It builds the quads and glutes with far less weight than a back squat.

Main musclesQuads, Glutes
EquipmentDumbbells, bench
TypeSingle-leg squat

Muscles worked

MainQuadsGlutes
HelpingAdductorsHamstrings

How to do it

  1. Stand about two feet in front of a bench and rest the top of your back foot on it.
  2. Hold a dumbbell in each hand at your sides.
  3. Lower straight down until your front thigh is about parallel to the floor and your back knee nearly touches it.
  4. Keep your front heel down and your front knee in line with your toes.
  5. Push through your front foot to stand back up.
  6. Finish all reps on one leg, then switch.

Common mistakes

  • Front foot too close to the bench. Your heel lifts and your knee jams forward. Step out until the bottom feels stable.
  • Pushing off the back leg. The back foot is just for balance. Keep the work on the front leg.
  • Short range. Go down until the back knee is just above the floor.

Sets and reps

  • Muscle: 3 sets of 8-12 reps per leg, stopping 1-3 reps short of failure.
  • Strength: 3-4 sets of 5-8 reps per leg.
  • Beginners: start with bodyweight, 2-3 sets of 8-10 per leg.

Questions

What muscles does the Bulgarian split squat work?

Mainly the quads and glutes of the front leg, with the adductors and hamstrings helping.

How do I work the glutes more?

Take a slightly longer stance and lean your torso forward a little. A shorter stance with an upright torso works the quads more.

Why is it so hard to balance?

Most people start with their feet in one line. Give yourself a little width between the front foot and the back foot, like standing on train tracks.
