Lat Pulldown

The lat pulldown is a cable pull from overhead. It builds the lats and upper back, and works as a lighter way to train toward pull-ups.

Main musclesLats
EquipmentCable pulldown machine
TypeVertical pull

Muscles worked

MainLats
HelpingBicepsRear deltsUpper back

How to do it

  1. Set the thigh pad so your legs are locked in snugly.
  2. Grip the bar a little wider than shoulder width, palms facing away.
  3. Sit down with your arms straight and lean back slightly.
  4. Pull the bar to your upper chest, driving your elbows down and back.
  5. Squeeze your lats at the bottom without leaning further back.
  6. Let the bar rise under control until your arms are straight.

Common mistakes

  • Leaning way back. Turns it into a row. Keep a small lean and pull down, not back.
  • Pulling behind the neck. Strains the shoulders for no extra benefit. Pull to the front.
  • Cutting the stretch at the top. Let your arms straighten fully to train the lats through their full range.

Sets and reps

  • Muscle: 3-4 sets of 6-12 reps, stopping 1-3 reps short of failure.
  • Beginners: 3 sets of 10-12 reps.

Questions

What muscles does the lat pulldown work?

Mainly the lats, with the biceps, rear delts and upper back helping.

Wide or close grip?

Both work the lats. A close or neutral grip lets the arms help more and often allows more weight; a wide grip shortens the range a little.

Is the lat pulldown as good as pull-ups?

For building the lats, yes. It's easier to load lightly and add weight in small jumps, which makes it a good way to work toward pull-ups.
