Barbell Hip Thrust

The hip thrust trains the glutes through a full hip extension with your upper back on a bench. It lets you load the glutes heavily without loading the spine.

Main musclesGlutes
EquipmentBarbell, bench, bar pad
TypeHip extension

Muscles worked

MainGlutes
HelpingHamstringsAdductorsQuads

How to do it

  1. Sit on the floor with your upper back against a bench, just below the shoulder blades.
  2. Roll a padded barbell over your hips.
  3. Plant your feet about hip width, close enough that your shins are vertical at the top.
  4. Tuck your chin and drive through your heels to lift your hips.
  5. Stop when your body is straight from shoulders to knees and squeeze your glutes.
  6. Lower under control until your hips are just above the floor.

Common mistakes

  • Arching the lower back at the top. Finish with the glutes, ribs down, not by bending your spine.
  • Feet too far out. Shifts the work to the hamstrings. Keep the shins vertical at lockout.
  • Rushing the top. Pause for a second at the top of each rep.

Sets and reps

  • Muscle: 3-4 sets of 6-12 reps, stopping 1-3 reps short of failure.
  • Strength: 3-4 sets of 5-8 reps.
  • Beginners: 3 sets of 10-12 reps with a light bar or bodyweight.

Questions

What muscles does the hip thrust work?

Mainly the glutes, with the hamstrings, adductors and quads helping.

Hip thrust or glute bridge?

The hip thrust uses a bench for a bigger range of motion and usually more load. The glute bridge is easier to set up and a good place to start.

The bar hurts my hips. What helps?

Use a thick bar pad or a folded mat, and set the bar in the crease of your hips rather than on the hip bones.
