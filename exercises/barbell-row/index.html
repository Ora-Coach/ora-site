Barbell Row

The bent-over barbell row is a heavy pull for the upper back and lats. It balances out pressing and helps with a strong, stable back for squats and deadlifts.

Main musclesLats, Upper back
EquipmentBarbell, plates
TypeCompound pull

Muscles worked

MainLatsUpper back
HelpingRear deltsBicepsLower back

How to do it

  1. Stand with feet hip width and grip the bar just outside your knees, palms facing you.
  2. Hinge at the hips until your torso is about 30-45° above the floor, knees slightly bent.
  3. Let the bar hang at arm's length with your back flat and neck neutral.
  4. Pull the bar to your lower ribs or upper belly, driving your elbows back.
  5. Squeeze your shoulder blades together at the top.
  6. Lower the bar under control without letting your torso rise.

Common mistakes

  • Standing up as you pull. Turns it into a shrug. Keep the same torso angle every rep.
  • Jerking the weight with the hips. A little body English on the last reps is fine; most reps should be strict.
  • Rounding the upper back. Keep the chest up and pull with the back, not just the arms.
  • Pulling to the neck. Aim for the lower ribs so the lats do more of the work.

Sets and reps

  • Muscle: 3-4 sets of 6-12 reps, stopping 1-3 reps short of failure.
  • Strength: 3-4 sets of 5-8 reps.
  • Beginners: 3 sets of 8-10 reps.

Questions

What muscles does the barbell row work?

The lats and the upper back (rhomboids and middle traps), with the rear delts and biceps helping and the lower back holding the hinge.

Overhand or underhand grip?

Overhand is the standard and hits the upper back a little more. Underhand lets the biceps help and often allows more weight.

How bent over should I be?

About 30-45° above the floor is common. More upright lets you lift more but works the lats less; a flat back (Pendlay row) is stricter.
