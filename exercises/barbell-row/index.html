Barbell Row

The bent-over barbell row is a heavy pull for the upper back and lats. It balances out pressing and builds a strong, stable back for squats and deadlifts.

How to Do It

1
GripFeet hip width, hands just outside your knees, palms facing you.
2
HingeTorso about 30-45° above the floor, knees slightly bent.
3
HangLet the bar hang at arm's length with a flat back and neutral neck.
4
PullBring the bar to your lower ribs, driving your elbows back.
5
SqueezePinch your shoulder blades together at the top.
6
LowerUnder control, without letting your torso rise.

Common Mistakes

Standing Up as You Pull
Keep the same torso angle every rep.
Heaving With the Hips
Most reps should be strict. A little body English on the last ones is fine.
Rounding the Upper Back
Keep your chest up and pull with your back, not just your arms.
Pulling to the Neck
Aim for the lower ribs so the lats do more work.

Questions

What muscles does the barbell row work?

The lats and the upper back (rhomboids and middle traps), with the rear delts and biceps helping and the lower back holding the hinge.

Overhand or underhand grip?

Overhand is the standard and hits the upper back a little more. Underhand lets the biceps help and often allows more weight.

How bent over should I be?

About 30-45° above the floor is common. More upright lets you lift more but works the lats less; a flat back (Pendlay row) is stricter.
