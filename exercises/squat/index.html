Barbell Back Squat

The back squat trains the whole lower body under a barbell. It is one of the best lifts for leg strength and size, and one of the three powerlifts.

Main musclesQuads, Glutes
EquipmentBarbell, squat rack
TypeCompound legs

Muscles worked

MainQuadsGlutes
HelpingAdductorsLower backCore

How to do it

  1. Set the bar in the rack at about mid-chest height. Step under it and rest it on your upper back, not your neck.
  2. Grip the bar just outside your shoulders and squeeze your upper back tight.
  3. Unrack, take two or three steps back, and stand with feet about shoulder width, toes turned out slightly.
  4. Take a big breath into your belly and brace as if about to be punched.
  5. Sit down and back, knees tracking over your toes, until your hip crease is at or below your knee.
  6. Drive up through your whole foot, keeping your chest up, and breathe out at the top.

Common mistakes

  • Knees caving in. Push your knees out over your toes on the way down and up.
  • Heels lifting. Keep your weight over mid-foot. Try a slightly wider stance or lifting shoes.
  • Cutting depth as the set goes on. Shallow reps make the weight look heavier than you are. Keep each rep the same depth.
  • Losing the brace. Re-brace before every rep, not just the first.

Sets and reps

  • Strength: 3-5 sets of 3-6 reps, stopping 1-3 reps short of failure.
  • Muscle: 3-4 sets of 6-12 reps, stopping 1-3 reps short of failure.
  • Beginners: 3 sets of 5-8 reps, 2-3 times a week.

Questions

What muscles do squats work?

Mostly the quads and glutes, with the adductors (inner thighs) helping and the lower back and core holding your torso upright.

How deep should I squat?

Go as deep as you can with a neutral lower back. For most people that is hip crease at or below the knee, which is also the powerlifting standard.

Is it bad if my knees go past my toes?

No. Knees moving forward is normal, especially for longer legs. What matters is that they track in line with your toes and your heels stay down.
