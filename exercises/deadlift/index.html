Barbell Deadlift

The deadlift picks a loaded bar up from the floor. It trains more muscle at once than almost any other lift and is the heaviest of the three powerlifts for most people.

Main musclesGlutes, Hamstrings, Lower back
EquipmentBarbell, plates
TypeCompound hinge

Muscles worked

MainGlutesHamstringsLower back
HelpingQuadsLats and upper backForearms (grip)Traps

How to do it

  1. Stand with mid-foot under the bar, feet about hip width.
  2. Hinge down and grip the bar just outside your legs.
  3. Bend your knees until your shins touch the bar. Keep your back flat and chest up.
  4. Pull your shoulders down and back to tighten your lats and take the slack out of the bar.
  5. Push the floor away. Keep the bar against your legs as it rises.
  6. Stand tall with hips and knees straight, then lower the bar the same way under control.

Common mistakes

  • Rounding the lower back. Lower the weight and keep the back flat from the first inch.
  • Bar drifting away from the legs. A bar out in front is much harder to lift. Keep it close the whole way.
  • Hips shooting up first. Your chest and hips should rise together. Push with the legs as you pull.
  • Leaning back at the top. Finish by standing straight, not by bending backward.

Sets and reps

  • Strength: 2-4 sets of 2-5 reps, stopping 1-3 reps short of failure.
  • Muscle: 3 sets of 5-8 reps. Long sets of deadlifts tire the grip and back fast.
  • Beginners: 1-3 sets of 5 reps, once or twice a week.

Questions

What muscles does the deadlift work?

The glutes, hamstrings and lower back do most of the work, with the quads, lats, upper back, traps and forearms helping.

Conventional or sumo?

Both are fine. Sumo uses a wide stance and a more upright torso; conventional has a narrower stance. Most people are stronger in one, so train the one that feels better.

Should I use straps?

Straps help on heavy back-off sets when grip gives out before your back and legs. Train some sets without them so your grip keeps up.
