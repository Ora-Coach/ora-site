Barbell Overhead Press

The overhead press pushes a barbell from your shoulders to straight arms overhead, standing. It builds the shoulders and triceps and a strong, braced core.

Main musclesFront delts, Side delts
EquipmentBarbell, rack
TypeVertical push

Muscles worked

MainFront deltsSide delts
HelpingTricepsUpper chestTrapsCore

How to do it

  1. Set the bar at upper-chest height. Grip it just outside your shoulders.
  2. Unrack with the bar resting on the front of your shoulders, elbows slightly in front of the bar.
  3. Stand with feet hip width. Squeeze your glutes and brace your core.
  4. Press the bar straight up, moving your head back slightly to let it pass.
  5. Once the bar passes your forehead, push your head forward under it and lock out over mid-foot.
  6. Lower under control back to your shoulders.

Common mistakes

  • Leaning way back. Turns it into an incline press and stresses the lower back. Glutes tight, ribs down.
  • Pressing the bar forward. The bar should travel in a straight line over mid-foot.
  • Not finishing the lockout. Push all the way up with the bar over your shoulders and mid-foot.

Sets and reps

  • Strength: 3-5 sets of 3-6 reps, stopping 1-3 reps short of failure.
  • Muscle: 3-4 sets of 6-12 reps, stopping 1-3 reps short of failure.
  • Beginners: 3 sets of 5-8 reps. Small jumps (2.5 lb or 1.25 kg plates) keep it moving.

Questions

What muscles does the overhead press work?

Mainly the front and side delts, with the triceps, upper chest and traps helping, and the core holding you upright.

Should I press standing or seated?

Standing trains the core and is the classic strength lift. Seated lets you focus on the shoulders and often use a little more weight.

Why is my overhead press so much weaker than my bench?

That's normal. The shoulders are smaller muscles than the chest, and many lifters press roughly 60-70% of what they bench.
