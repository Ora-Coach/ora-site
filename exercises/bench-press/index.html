Barbell Bench Press

The bench press is the classic upper-body strength lift. Done well, it builds the chest, shoulders and triceps and lets you add weight for years.

Main musclesChest
EquipmentBarbell, flat bench, rack
TypeCompound push

Muscles worked

MainChest
HelpingFront deltsTriceps

How to do it

  1. Lie on the bench with your eyes under the bar. Plant your feet flat on the floor.
  2. Pull your shoulder blades back and down, and keep a slight arch in your upper back.
  3. Grip the bar a little wider than shoulder width, wrists stacked over your elbows.
  4. Unrack and hold the bar over your shoulders with your arms straight.
  5. Lower the bar under control to your lower chest, elbows about 45-70° from your body.
  6. Touch your chest without bouncing, then press up and slightly back to the start.

Common mistakes

  • Elbows flared straight out. Puts more stress on the shoulders. Tuck them a little so the forearms stay vertical.
  • Bouncing off the chest. Hides weak reps and can bruise the sternum. Touch, then press.
  • Butt lifting off the bench. Shortens the range and breaks competition rules. Drive through your feet, not your hips.
  • Shoulders rolling forward. Keep the shoulder blades pinned for the whole set.

Sets and reps

  • Strength: 3-5 sets of 3-6 reps, stopping 1-3 reps short of failure.
  • Muscle: 3-4 sets of 6-12 reps, stopping 1-3 reps short of failure.
  • Beginners: 3 sets of 5-8 reps, adding a little weight when every rep looks the same.

Questions

What muscles does the bench press work?

Mainly the chest (pecs), with the front of the shoulders and the triceps helping. The upper back and lats keep the bar path steady.

Should the bar touch my chest?

Yes, for a full rep. Lower it to the lower chest or just below the nipple line, touch without bouncing, and press.

How wide should my grip be?

Most people do best a little wider than shoulder width, with forearms vertical at the bottom. Very wide grips shorten the range and stress the shoulders.
