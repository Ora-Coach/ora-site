The RPE scale

RPE (rating of perceived exertion) rates a set by how many more good reps you could have done. This is the reps-in-reserve version used in strength training.

RPEReps leftWhat it feels like
100Max effort. No more reps possible.
9.50No more reps, but a little more weight might have gone.
91One more rep in the tank.
8.51-2Definitely one more, maybe two.
82Two more reps in the tank.
7.52-3Definitely two more, maybe three.
73Three more reps. The bar moves fast.
5-64-6Warm-up or technique weight.
1-4LotsVery light to light effort.

How to use RPE in training

  • Pick a target, not just a weight. "5 reps at RPE 8" means a set of 5 that leaves about 2 in the tank. The chart gives the starting weight.
  • Adjust after the first set. If 5 reps felt like RPE 9, take a little off. If they felt like RPE 7, add a little. That's the point of RPE: it follows how strong you are that day.
  • Rate it right away. Say the number as soon as you rack the bar, before you talk yourself up or down.
  • Expect to get better at it. Most people are unsure at first. Ratings near failure (8-10) tend to be the most accurate.

How the chart is built

Each cell uses the Epley formula with reps in reserve, the same math as our 1RM calculator and the Ora app:

% of 1RM = 100 ÷ (1 + (reps + RIR) ÷ 30)

RIR is 10 − RPE. A single at RPE 10 is 100%, your max as lifted. Other published RPE charts can differ by a percent or two, since each comes from a different formula or data set. Use the chart for your first set, then adjust.

Questions

What does RPE 8 mean?

RPE 8 means you finished the set with about 2 more good reps in you. On this chart, 5 reps at RPE 8 is about 81% of your one rep max.

What is the difference between RPE and RIR?

They describe the same thing from two sides. RIR (reps in reserve) counts the reps you had left, and RPE rates the effort out of 10. For hard sets, RPE = 10 − RIR, so 2 reps in reserve is RPE 8.

What RPE should I train at?

Most programs keep the bulk of their working sets around RPE 7-9, a few reps short of failure, with all-out sets used sparingly. Lighter technique work can sit around RPE 6.

Is RPE 10 the same as failing a rep?

Not quite. RPE 10 means you couldn't have done another rep with good form. You finished the last one; there just wasn't another in you.

How do I get better at rating RPE?

Write down the RPE right after each set and compare it with what happens on later sets. People tend to judge reps left more accurately the closer they are to failure, so ratings of 8-10 are usually more reliable than ratings of 6.

Why do RPE charts give different percentages?

Each chart comes from a different formula or data set, so they can differ by a percent or two. Treat any chart as a starting weight and adjust from how the first set feels.
