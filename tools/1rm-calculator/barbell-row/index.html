How to calculate your barbell row 1RM

You need one hard set: the weight, the reps, and how many more reps you could have done.

  1. After warming up, do a hard set of 2-6 good reps on the barbell row.
  2. Note how many reps you had left in the tank (RIR). RPE 8 means about 2 left.
  3. Add the reps you did to the reps you had left, divide by 30 and add 1, then multiply by the weight.

Example: 155 lb for 5 reps at RPE 8 (2 left): 155 × (1 + (5 + 2) ÷ 30) = 155 × 1.233 ≈ 191 lb. If those 5 reps were all-out, it’s 155 × (1 + 5 ÷ 30) ≈ 181 lb. In kilograms, 70 kg for 5 reps at RPE 8 comes to about 86.5 kg.

Getting a good row estimate

  • Use the "Plan a set" table. Rows are usually trained for 6-12 reps. Pick the reps you want and how many to leave in the tank, and the table gives the weight.
  • Keep the body still. Reps where you heaved with your hips or stood up taller make the estimate read high. Count reps that touched the same spot with the same torso angle.
  • Pendlay rows and bent-over rows differ. A Pendlay row starts from the floor each rep with the torso about flat. Estimate each one on its own.
  • Treat it as a guide. Most 1RM formula research used the bench press, squat and deadlift, so a row estimate is rougher.

Warm up for a max attempt

Testing a max row is rare and you don't need to. If you do, ramp up like this. Rest 2-3 minutes before the heavier sets and 3-5 before the attempt.

How the formulas compare

Different formulas give slightly different answers. For sets under about 10 reps they stay within a few percent of each other.

How the calculator works

The main estimate uses the Epley formula with reps in reserve, the same math Ora uses in the app:

1RM = weight × (1 + (reps + RIR) ÷ 30)

RIR (reps in reserve) is how many more reps you could have done. If you pick an RPE, RIR is 10 − RPE, so a set at RPE 8 counts as about 2 reps left. If you pick "Not sure", the calculator assumes the set was all-out, which gives a conservative estimate. A true single at RPE 10 is your max as lifted.

Questions

How do I calculate my barbell row 1 rep max?

Take a hard set, add the reps you did to the reps you had left, divide by 30 and add 1, then multiply by the weight. For example, 155 lb for 5 reps with 2 left is 155 × (1 + 7 ÷ 30) ≈ 191 lb. The calculator above does this for you.

Do I need to know my barbell row max?

Not really. It mostly helps you pick working weights, which the "Plan a set" table does from any recent set.

What is a Pendlay row?

A barbell row where the bar starts on the floor each rep and your torso stays about parallel to the floor. It usually moves a bit less than a bent-over row with a more upright torso.

Do cheat reps count?

They inflate the estimate. Count reps where your torso angle stayed the same and the bar came up by pulling, not by hip drive.

How many reps should I do on barbell rows?

Most programs use 6-12 reps for rows, a few reps short of failure. Use the table to find the weight for the reps you want.

What is RPE and RIR?

RPE rates how hard a set was on a 1-10 scale. RIR (reps in reserve) is how many more reps you could have done. RPE 8 means about 2 reps in reserve.

How accurate is a 1RM calculator?

It's an estimate. 1RM formulas are most accurate for sets of about 10 reps or fewer taken close to failure. Your true max on a given day can be a few percent higher or lower.
