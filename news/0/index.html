Ora Updates · #0 · November 25, 2026

What this is, and how to try Ora now

This is the first issue of Ora Updates, a short email I'll send every two weeks with what's new in Ora and one training idea you can actually use in the gym. I'm Eric, a CS student at UF, and I build Ora, an AI training coach for lifting.

A bit of background: I started lifting back in my senior year of high school, and I tried building a workout app for it in Swift, but it didn't go anywhere since I wasn't really well-versed in app building yet. Fast-forward to UF, I came back to the idea, and Ora is what came out of it.

So here's where Ora is today. Orbi, our AI coach, plans your training, but the actual weights come from tested progression rules built from published strength research, not from a chatbot guessing. After every set, Ora looks at how hard it felt and sets up the next one, and you can tap Why on any set to see exactly where the number came from. On top of that, every workout earns XP, so you climb ranks from Newbie to Olympus, unlock badges and titles, and can duel a friend for a week.

The beta is open right now. On iPhone you can join through TestFlight, and on Android you can get it on Google Play. Ora officially launches on the App Store and Google Play on November 30th, and people who join early get a Founding Member price, which I'll go over in the launch email.

This issue's training idea

Training close to failure matters for building muscle, but going all the way to failure on every set usually isn't worth the extra fatigue. Stopping with about 1-3 reps left in the tank is a good default for most of your working sets, and you can save the all-out efforts for the last set of an exercise.

If you try the beta, email support@oracoach.app and tell me what you think, good or bad. I read every message, and feedback from testers has shaped a ton of what's in Ora so far.

Eric
